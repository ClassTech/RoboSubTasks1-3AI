RoboSub Tasks 1–3, flown by learned skills

Code on GitHub →

A simulated submarine starts in front of the divided gate, facing a random way. It goes through one side of the gate, then slaloms through three sets of poles on that same side, turns around, and comes back the same way. The scripted mission is hand-written code. The learned mission is four small neural networks that were trained in the simulator.

Gate→Dance→Slalom 1→2→3→Turn around→3→2→1→Gate

Three runs from the same start

All three start from the same position and heading (test seed 10001). The videos play at 2× real time.

Scripted mission

This is RoboSim's hand-written mission, adapted to this sub. A state machine finds the gate in the camera image, picks the side nearer the image center, and lines up on it. It stops to stabilize between tasks. It has no dance.

This run: success, 137 s
300 test starts: 281 (93.7%), typically ~134 s

Learned mission

Three learned skills fly this run: gate, slalom (all three sets) and turnaround. A runner just names them in order. A small learned detector decides when each skill is finished. Ten times a second, the active skill picks a turn, a depth and a forward thrust, and the sub's own control loops fly them.

This run: success, 119 s
300 test starts: 289 (96.3%), typically ~119 s

With the victory dance

After the gate, a fourth learned skill does the dance. It turns a full circle one way and then back, stopping at each quarter, then rolls all the way over to the right and back to the left. The judges count 16 quarter turns in all. It then levels out, comes back to depth on its starting heading, and the slalom skill takes over.

This run: success, 204 s, 16 of 16 stops
300 test starts: 287 (95.7%), typically ~224 s; 78% scored all 16 stops

How much computing the training took

≈ 50 hon one desktop

The training ran on a single 10-core Intel i9-10910 iMac, with no GPU. Each training run drove 9 simulators in parallel, and usually two runs shared the machine. That keeps all its cores busy for about 50 hours, or roughly 500 core-hours.

This is an estimate from the training logs of the 68 PPO runs still on disk (94 run-hours, 47 hours of machine time) plus 2 hours of dance imitation. It leaves out the done detectors and the scoring, which took minutes each. Most of the time went to experiments. The chain of runs behind the final policies took about 20 hours.

PPO alone never learned the dance well. Eight short rounds of imitating a hand-written controller did, in about 2 hours.

The simulator runs at 60 Hz, with the sub's measured thrust (7.85 N per thruster), drag, thruster ramp-up, and a noisy camera, pressure sensor and IMU. The course follows ClassTech's RoboSim. Videos were recorded with tools/record_run.py. The code, the simulator and the training setup are on GitHub: github.com/ClassTech/RoboSubTasks1-3AI.
